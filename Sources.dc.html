Sources & notes

The four records behind André's journey — how reliable each is, and where the family memoir and the official paper trail disagree.

FAMILY ORAL HISTORY

Family memoir

Handwritten account, author unnamed — written by a descendant's wife. Written well after the events described, likely decades later, based on family recollection rather than contemporaneous records. Warmest and most detailed on the early Russian, Siberian and Balkan period, which no official record corroborates.

OFFICIAL GOVERNMENT RECORD

Metropolitan Police Special Branch report on naturalisation application

15 October 1935 · Ref. H.O.2829, 350/N.K./980 (HO 405/26764)

Based on a formal interview with André himself plus corroborating referees, bank records and police files. The most contemporaneous and fact-checked source, but focused on residence, character and finances relevant to naturalisation — it is silent on Siberia and on any 1905 exile beyond a term of imprisonment.

PRIMARY SOURCE DOCUMENT

Serbian "Cross of Mercy" (Krst milosrđa) certificate

Issued 25 August 1915, in the name of King Peter I of Serbia · Ref. F.M. No. 12643

Awarded for care of wounded and sick soldiers in the wars of 1912 and 1913 — this predates WWI service and may relate to the Balkan Wars, or be a general-service Red Cross–type award issued during his 1915 Serbian service.

CURATED PUBLIC RECORD

Imperial War Museums, "Lives of the First World War"

Aggregates a Medal Index Card, British Red Cross volunteer transcription, marriage index, 1939 Register, Mabel Stobart's own published account, and the National Archives naturalisation certificate reference. Licensed under the IWM Non-Commercial Licence — review before public reproduction.
